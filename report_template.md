## Abstract

Industrial defect classifiers are limited by the scarcity of labelled defect images. This project investigates whether synthetic images produced with a pretrained latent diffusion model can improve a lightweight classifier trained on very little real data from the NEU surface defect database (six hot-rolled steel defect classes). {{BACKEND}} Images were automatically inspected and filtered ({{N_KEPT}} of {{N_GEN}} generated images were kept) and used to augment a limited real training set of 30 images per class. Five configurations were evaluated on the same held-out real test set ({{N_TEST}} images) using {{FEATURES}} features and an RBF-SVM: full real data, limited real data, limited real plus synthetic, synthetic only, and a classical-augmentation control. All numbers in this report are produced by the project code from the saved result files. The project is a CPU-only, reduced-scale study motivated by SynSur (Kühn et al., 2026) and does not replicate it.

## 1. Introduction

Automated visual inspection needs many labelled examples of every defect type, yet defects are rare and annotation needs expert knowledge. Generative models offer a way to enlarge scarce datasets. Latent diffusion models (LDMs) are attractive because they were designed to make high-quality image synthesis feasible with limited compute (Rombach et al., 2022). This project asks a narrow, testable question: *does adding filtered LDM-generated images to a small real training set change classification performance on real test images?*

## 2. Problem Statement

Given a six-class steel-surface defect classification task with only 30 labelled real images per class, determine whether additional synthetic images generated by a pretrained latent diffusion model (without fine-tuning, on CPU hardware) improve accuracy and macro-F1 on a held-out set of real images, compared with (i) the same limited real data alone and (ii) the same data plus classical augmentation.

## 3. Research Objectives

## 4. Literature Review

Generative adversarial networks were an early route to defect synthesis; diffusion models have since become a strong alternative for conditional image generation. Rombach et al. (2022) introduced latent diffusion models, which apply the denoising process in the latent space of a pretrained autoencoder and add cross-attention for conditioning on text or other inputs, reducing training and inference cost relative to pixel-space diffusion. Kühn et al. (2026) apply diffusion-based synthesis specifically to industrial surface defects and evaluate the effect on downstream detection (Section 5). Classical features such as histograms of oriented gradients (Dalal and Triggs, 2005) combined with support-vector machines (Cortes and Vapnik, 1995) remain strong, cheap baselines for small texture datasets, which is why they are used here.

## 5. Selected Research Paper: SynSur

Kühn et al. (2026), *SynSur: An end-to-end generative pipeline for synthetic industrial surface defect generation and detection* (arXiv:2604.26633, submitted 29 April 2026), present an end-to-end pipeline that, according to the paper's abstract and summaries of it, combines vision-language-model-based prompt extraction, LoRA-adapted diffusion (Stable Diffusion), mask-guided inpainting, and sample filtering (using DreamSim and CLIPScore) with automatic derivation of annotations. It is evaluated on BSData (pitting defects on ball screw drives) and the scratch subset of MSD, training the detectors YOLOX, YOLOv26 and LW-DETR under real-only, synthetic-only, mixed and union regimes, starting from a candidate pool of 1,000 generated images. The authors conclude that diffusion-based synthesis is most valuable for strengthening scarce real datasets rather than substituting for them. *We did not re-derive the paper's numerical results and report none of them here; the description above should be checked against the paper before submission.*

## 6. Latent Diffusion Background

An LDM first compresses images with a pretrained autoencoder into a lower-dimensional latent space, then trains a denoising diffusion model there. Generation starts from noise in latent space, iteratively denoises it under conditioning (e.g. a text embedding injected by cross-attention), and decodes the result back to pixels. In image-to-image mode, the starting latent is the encoded input image partially noised according to a *strength* parameter, so higher strength gives images that depart further from the input. Stable Diffusion is the widely available LDM family used here.

## 7. Dataset Description

The NEU surface defect database (Song and Yan, 2013) contains grayscale 200×200 images of six defect types on hot-rolled steel strips: crazing, inclusion, patches, pitted surface, rolled-in scale and scratches, with about 300 images per class (1,800 in total). Images were split once, stratified by class, into 70% train / 15% validation / 15% test with a fixed seed. The test split was never used for training, hyper-parameter selection, prompting or filtering.

## 8. Methodology

**Pipeline.** (1) Prepare and split data. (2) Generate synthetic images. (3) Inspect and filter them. (4) Train classifiers for configurations A–E. (5) Evaluate on the real test set. (6) Produce figures and this report.

**Configurations.** A: full real training split. B: 30 real images per class (fixed random subset). C: B plus filtered diffusion-generated images. D: filtered synthetic images only. E (control): B plus the same number of classically augmented copies of B (flips, rotations, crops, brightness/contrast, noise), added so that any gain in C can be compared with a cheap non-generative alternative. E is *not* diffusion.

**Generation.** {{BACKEND}} Each class has its own descriptive prompt (e.g. "long thin straight scratch lines across the metal" for scratches); prompts are stored in `src/generate_synthetic.py` and in the manifest for every image.

**Filtering.** An image is kept only if it is not blank or saturated, is neither a near-copy of nor unrelated to its source image (structural similarity thresholds), and a logistic-regression judge trained only on the 30-per-class real subset assigns the intended class a probability of at least 0.40. This is a lightweight CPU substitute for SynSur's DreamSim/CLIPScore filtering, not an equivalent.

## 10. Implementation Details

Python 3 with scikit-learn, scikit-image, Pillow, matplotlib; PyTorch and Hugging Face `diffusers` only for generation. Everything runs on CPU. Each stage writes a marker file and skips finished work on re-run; generated images are appended to a manifest so generation can resume after interruption. Source code is in `main.py` and `src/`.

## 15. Ethical Considerations

Synthetic images can look authentic; they must be labelled as synthetic in datasets and never mixed into evaluation sets, which is why the test split here contains only real images. Models trained on generated defects may fail on defect types the generator renders poorly, creating a risk of missed defects in safety-relevant products; synthetic data should supplement, not replace, real validation. Pretrained generators carry biases and licensing terms from their training data, and the diffusion model's licence should be checked before any commercial use. Compute and energy use were kept low by avoiding fine-tuning and by generating a small number of images.

## 16. Limitations and Challenges

- **LoRA fine-tuning was not performed** (not feasible on CPU in the available time), unlike SynSur; a general-purpose pretrained model may not reproduce steel-texture statistics faithfully.
- Image-to-image generation stays close to its source image, so diversity is limited by the 30-per-class seed set.
- Generation resolution is low and the model was prompted with hand-written text; no inpainting, masks or vision-language prompts were used.
- The filter's class-consistency check favours images that a small model already finds easy, which can bias the kept set.
- The test set is small ({{N_TEST}} images), so confidence intervals are wide; one limited subset and one split seed were used, so results may vary with other seeds.
- NEU is a relatively easy benchmark, and the full-data classifier may already be near its ceiling.

## 17. Lessons Learned

Reporting confidence intervals prevented over-reading small differences. Including a classical-augmentation control was necessary to show whether diffusion adds anything beyond cheap transformations. Keeping generation, filtering and evaluation as separate resumable stages made CPU-only experimentation practical.

## 18. Conclusion

We built and evaluated a complete CPU-only pipeline for diffusion-based synthetic defect augmentation on NEU. The measured effect of synthetic data on classification is reported above with uncertainty; claims in this report are limited to what those measurements support. Future work: LoRA fine-tuning on a GPU, mask-guided inpainting for localised defects, stronger perceptual filters (DreamSim, CLIPScore), and repeating the study over multiple seeds and subsets.
